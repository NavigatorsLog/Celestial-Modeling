## 2. Decision rule (committed)
Each run is read at t*, the first progress row at which the small-body pool (mass in bodies no larger than one planetesimal, 0.0093 Earth masses) is at or below 0.75 of its starting value [stage set by CBH, 5 October 2026]. S = fragment mass from stripping events up to t*, divided by the starting disk mass (2.604 Earth masses); a stripping event is a collision that made fragments while the target lost mass or the projectile survived. F = stripping events divided by resolved collisions up to t*. The unit is the run pair; intervals are percentile bootstrap, 10,000 draws over pairs, seed 20261004.

> **SUPPORTED iff** the 90 percent interval of R = (sum of S at factor 3) / (sum of S at factor 1) lies inside 0.8 to 1.25, AND the 90 percent interval of dF = mean over pairs of F(factor 3) minus F(factor 1) lies inside minus 0.05 to plus 0.05; this verdict is labelled ACCEPTED. **Otherwise a registered null**, labelled as follows. If either interval excludes no difference (R = 1 or dF = 0): NOT ACCEPTED (difference shown) [rule v2, approved by CBH]. Else, if the same-condition control also fails the rule: INCONCLUSIVE. Else: NOT ACCEPTED.
> n = 20 pairs. Statistics = R and dF. What null looks like numerically: R interval reaching outside 0.8 to 1.25, or dF interval reaching outside plus or minus 0.05.

Primary hypothesis (pinned):
- **H1:** inflating radii 3 times leaves collisional mantle stripping unchanged, within the margins, up to the matched stage. Supported iff the rule above returns ACCEPTED.

## 7. Literature situation
- Named open question I address: Childs and Steffen (2022, MNRAS 511:1848) used expansion factors of 3 and above only, for compute reasons, and found merger rates, orbital spacing, radial mixing and water content all change with the factor; whether factor 3 matches true size with fragmentation is untested.
- Named proven limit that bounds me: the earlier finding they cite (Kokubo and Ida 2002) that factors up to 10 change little holds only when the bodies' random speeds are not dominated by gravitational scattering. An acceptance here covers only the stage tested, not the late giant-impact phase, and the margins were chosen by Claude, not derived from what test F3 can tolerate.
